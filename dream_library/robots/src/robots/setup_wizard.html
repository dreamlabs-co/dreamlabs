Find your arms

We name each arm's adapter by its serial number, or by its USB port when it has none of its own (two adapters that report the same one), so a replug or a reboot cannot swap your arms: keep an adapter named by its port in that port. Only adapters your robot's arms can be on are listed.

Your arm is not listed?

Type its address the way your arm's driver opens it: an IP address, a CAN link, a serial port. Add each arm that is not listed. The wizard opens it through the driver like the ones it found, and the checks below run on what the driver reads.

    Arm settings

    Find arms opens each ticked adapter, and its arm goes limp so you can move it by hand: hold each arm, or rest it on its stand. Untick an adapter that belongs to something else (another robot on this computer) and it is left alone. Then move one arm a little, and say below which arm it is.

    Keep the names after a reboot

    Install this rule once. It names each arm's link after its adapter's serial number, and the names take effect at the next reboot. Don't unplug the adapters: a replug resets the arms' CAN bus.

    Your cameras

    Click the role each camera has. Then compare each one with a reference picture.

    The data you recorded on this robot for your fine-tune, as a folder on this computer. Its first frames become the pictures each camera is compared with, so you can line your cameras up with the views your model learned from. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is saved.

    Your camera is not listed?

    Type any address OpenCV opens: a network camera's stream (rtsp://192.168.1.50/stream1), a /dev/video path. For a camera only its maker's SDK reads, type a class of your own instead, as module:Class or my_cams.py:Class. Class(camera) opens it, read() returns (ok, frame) with a BGR picture, and release() lets it go.

    Home pose

    Put each arm in its home pose, the pose every run starts from, then press OK.

    Seat each arm like this: folded down onto its base, the wrist camera facing up, the gripper closed.

    The drawing is your arms as your driver reads them, on dream's model of the arm. If it does not look like your real arms, a joint's zero, direction or units is off: fix that first. Drag a picture to turn it.

    Your grippers' range

    The grippers are limp too. Open both grippers fully by hand and press Record open. Then close both fully and press Record closed. Each gripper will then open to just short of where it stopped, so its motor never pushes into the stop.

    Reach area

    Move each hand over the whole area it should work in.

    Paint one solid area in front of the arm, and stop short of your camera mount so it stays outside the edge. dream pulls the hand's targets into the area, and if the arm still heads out past the edge, it stops there for the rest of that move.

    5

    Move under power

    The arms move slowly: each joint a little, then each hand. Check that the real arms move like the drawings.

    Fix:

    Did each joint and the hand move like the drawing?

    Each joint the same way, in the same order, and the hand the same way in the sweep. A joint that turned the other way, or two joints that traded places, is a No.

    Choose your driver file

    A .py file on this computer (the robot's, where the wizard runs). Open folders until you find it.

    Skip the tool center point?

    Not advised. Dream-1 moves your gripper by this point, so without it hand-position actions may land off target. Your arm file records that you skipped it, and every start warns.

    Are you sure?

    Skipping this step without passing the checks can meaningfully degrade how your robot performs.

    Are you sure?

    Without a reach area the arms can move anywhere, including into your camera mount. Painting it makes runs safer and gives better results.
